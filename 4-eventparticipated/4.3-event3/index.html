Event 3

Summary Report: “Amazon Web Services (AWS): Enterprise Cloud Architectures and Industry Applications – Featuring Cloud Kinetics & Renova Cloud”

Date & Time: 09:00 – 12:00, 07/04/2026   |   Location: 26th Floor, Bitexco Financial Tower – AWS Office, Ho Chi Minh City

Event Objectives

  • Share enterprise cloud architecture patterns and real-world industry applications (finance, aviation, retail, manufacturing, etc.).
  • Connect students/trainees with AWS and its implementation partners (Cloud Kinetics, Renova Cloud) through real career stories.
  • Provide a candid perspective on the Cloud/Data job market in the AI era, along with a career development roadmap for new graduates.

Speakers

  • Mr. Nguyen Gia Hung – Head of Solutions Architect (Vietnam & Cambodia), Amazon Web Services
  • Mr. Banh Cam Vinh – Data Engineer, Renova Cloud
  • Ms. Nhu Tran – Account Manager, Amazon Web Services
  • Mr. Khang Nguyen – Solutions Architect, Cloud Kinetics (Swinburne University alumnus)

Key Highlights

1. Mr. Nguyen Gia Hung — “Job Market & Career Trends”

Following the presented agenda (Vietnam’s Cloud Market, A Decade of AWS Investment, Who’s Hiring, In-Demand Roles & Salaries, Skills Gap & Certification Path, Career Roadmap), Mr. Hung painted a full picture of Vietnam’s cloud market through the “A Decade of Investment, 2015–2026” slide: from the first Vietnamese enterprise customer (2015) to over 4,000 active customers (2020), the launch of the AWS Local Zone, First Cloud AI Journey, and new AI services (Bedrock, Kiro, QuickSuite) in the Vietnamese market.

  • The real hiring market: Companies no longer hand-hold new hires; expectations for fresh graduates are extremely strict (e.g., an intern failing an interview for not knowing Kubernetes). A great GPA without hands-on skill still makes it hard to land a job.
  • “Cloud First” and the AI trend: AWS revenue grew roughly 20x in 6 years. AI is reducing demand for Junior hires while increasing demand for Seniors, pushing students to use AI to mature faster and turn university projects into “production-ready” products (secure, AI-integrated, scalable).
  • Job-search strategy: Don’t aim for just a “Role” (e.g., Data Engineer) — aim for a specific “Industry” (finance, retail, manufacturing, etc.) and build projects that solve that industry’s actual pain points.
  • The success formula: ~90% of great jobs are never publicly posted — they come through internal referrals. His formula: Outcome = Capability × Visibility × Consistency. You must engage with the community to build your visibility.

2. Mr. Banh Cam Vinh — Data Engineer, Renova Cloud

  • The gap between school and reality: At school, data is usually clean, requirements are clear, timelines are long, and the consequence of a mistake is just failing a course. In a company, data is messy and comes from many sources, requirements change constantly, time pressure is real, and a system outage directly hits company revenue.
  • Leveling up personal projects: 90% of student projects only cover the “visible” 10% (pulling data, building a dashboard). Companies need you to handle the other 90%: error handling when data pipelines fail, security, and data quality. He recommends using AI to scan your code and generate a production-standard checklist for your own projects.
  • “One framework for every platform”: Instead of memorizing individual tools, master the “DNA of a Data Platform” (Ingestion, Processing, Storage, etc.). Understanding the core architecture lets you easily plug in any tool (AWS, open source) into a system.
  • AI cannot replace thinking: Even though AI can write code or build a dashboard, humans still hold the core responsibilities: understanding the business problem, making architectural decisions, and communicating across departments.

3. Ms. Nhu Tran — Account Manager, AWS

  • Overcoming fear: The two biggest fears are fear of making mistakes (fear of consequences, being judged) and fear of public speaking. She overcame these by treating public speaking as an “internship” — practicing repeatedly to get used to the pressure and steady her heart rate.
  • Communication and “small talk”: Miscommunication often stems from differing perspectives (e.g., a Builder wants to create value, a Seller wants to communicate value). She advises students to practice small talk — greeting managers or professors in the elevator or hallway — since you can’t expect people to remember you if you never proactively build your own visibility.
  • Finding opportunities (Blue Ocean): People differ in the opportunities they get. Instead of submitting applications into a crowded “Red Ocean,” find a way to stand out. She shared that she failed Amazon’s interview process 10 times before finally being hired on the 11th attempt — thanks to a former manager who referred her directly to the hiring manager because they valued her work attitude.

4. Mr. Khang Nguyen — “AI-Ready Freshers: Skills and Mindset in the AI Era”

  • Using AI requires a strong foundation: He warned against students over-relying on AI to do their work — you “can outsource the thinking, but not the understanding.” AI is only an amplifier: a strong foundation lets AI help you do more projects, while a weak foundation lets AI lead you to wrong conclusions.
  • A learning mindset: School is “paying to be allowed to make mistakes”; work is “being paid to not make mistakes.” Always go beyond the school’s grading rubric (if the curriculum requires 10 modules, study 13) and finish a project properly even when the extra part earns no additional credit.
  • Choosing your first job: Don’t just look at the salary — look at the 5 circles of benefit: Experience, Exposure, Network, Knowledge, and Personal Growth. A lower-paying job with rich experience can be an excellent springboard later on.
  • Fresher hiring criteria: For candidates without much experience, employers rank Attitude as the top factor, ahead of Skill Level, Exposure, Experience, and Aptitude. A willingness to ask “why” and persistence are the keys.

Key Takeaways

  • The Cloud/Data hiring market is clearly shifting toward hands-on skills and a mindset of using AI as a capability amplifier, not a substitute for genuine understanding.
  • An effective career strategy is to target a specific industry and solve its real pain points, rather than chasing a generic job title.
  • Most great opportunities come from internal referrals — visibility within the community and consistency matter as much as technical skill.
  • A “production-ready” personal project must handle the hidden 90%: security, error handling, and data quality — not just visualizing results.
  • Attitude and a growth mindset (not being afraid to ask “why,” persistence) are valued by employers even more than experience when hiring Freshers.

Applying to Work

  • Reviewing the CloudBrief final project through a “production-ready” lens: reinforcing error handling, security (already part of the Week 9 design), and validating input/output data quality.
  • Repositioning CloudBrief not as a generic Cloud Engineer project, but as a solution to a specific pain point (technology information overload), making the project story more compelling in interviews.
  • Proactively building “visibility” by continuing to write blog posts and attend AWS Study Group VN community events after the internship ends.
  • Applying the “study beyond the rubric” mindset — exploring AWS services beyond what the Bootcamp requires to be better prepared for real-world work.

Event Experience

This was the most substantive event I attended during my internship, because it went beyond technical knowledge and addressed the most pressing questions a soon-to-graduate student actually has: how tough the hiring market really is, how to stand out among hundreds of applications, and how AI is reshaping job requirements. Mr. Banh Cam Vinh’s point about the “hidden 90%” of a data project pushed me to look at how I’m building CloudBrief with a more critical eye. Ms. Nhu Tran’s story of failing Amazon’s interviews 10 times before getting hired was also a huge source of motivation — failure doesn’t mean stopping.
