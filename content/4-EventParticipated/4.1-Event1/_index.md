# Summary Report: "First Cloud AI Journey – Workshop & Community Day"

**Date & Time:** 09:00, 05/30/2026 &emsp; | &emsp; **Location:** 26th Floor, Bitexco Financial Tower – AWS Office, Ho Chi Minh City

### Event Objectives

- Create a space for First Cloud AI Journey (FCAJ) trainees to share personal projects, technical knowledge, and soft skills with one another.
- Introduce free tools and platforms that help beginners learn AWS.
- Launch and encourage trainees to join the program's internal Hackathon.
- Connect the FCAJ trainee community across cohorts, building motivation and inspiration for learning.

### Presentation Topics

1. **AWS Cloud Quest – A Free Hands-on Learning Playground** – introducing a free, game-based platform for learning AWS Cloud Practitioner skills
2. **Introducing the FCAJ Hackathon** – team "The Ballers" (Huynh An Khuong, Mai Quoc Anh, Nguyen Tran Minh Quan) – FCAJ Challenger
3. **"Why We Always Need Confidence"** – Nguyen Thi Quynh Nhu
4. **"Tử vi Đại Việt" (Vietnamese Astrology – AI Powered)** – Nghia Tran, Founder
5. **"The Hidden Iceberg of a Project: DevOps Before Disaster"** – Tran Minh Quan
6. **"The Iceberg of Procrastination"** – Khac Uy Pham, 3rd-year student, Vietnamese-German University

### Key Highlights

#### Part 1 – AWS Cloud Quest: Learning AWS Through a Game

The speaker introduced **AWS Cloud Quest**, a free learning platform that helps beginners get familiar with AWS through role-playing-style "quests" instead of dry theoretical documentation. Three key highlights: **Guided AWS Practice**, **Easy for Beginners**, and **Learning Through Game Quests** — lowering the barrier to entry for learning cloud computing.

![AWS Cloud Quest - A Free Hands-on Learning Playground](/images/4-EventParticipated/4.1-Event1/1-cloud-quest.jpg)

#### Part 2 – Introducing the FCAJ Hackathon

The **"The Ballers"** team (FCAJ Challenger) shared what a Hackathon is, why one should join, and recounted their real experience with humorous but honest keywords: *"tons of fun," "and bug fixes," "and back pains," "and sleep deprivation"* — capturing the true spirit of grinding through a project overnight while staying passionate. The talk closed with an invitation for other trainees to join the program's upcoming Hackathon.

#### Part 3 – Why We Always Need Confidence

Speaker Nguyen Thi Quynh Nhu presented on the importance of confidence for one's career and student life, following the agenda: **Let's Talk Confidence** → **The Cost of Self-Doubt in Student Life** → **The Science Behind the Struggle** → **The Power of Confidence** → **How to "Hack" Everyday Confidence** → Wrap-up.

#### Part 4 – Tử vi Đại Việt (Vietnamese Astrology – AI Powered)

**Nghia Tran**, the project's Founder, introduced his personal product **"Tử vi Đại Việt"** — an AI-powered Vietnamese astrology platform with the tagline *"Decode your destiny – Understand your inner self,"* with content generated by AI and verified by experts. Notably, Nghia Tran shared the AWS architecture he is currently trialing to prepare for moving the website to the cloud, while also weighing whether running on AWS would cost more than his current traditional server hosting before making a final migration decision.

![Tử vi Đại Việt - Vietnamese Astrology AI Powered](/images/4-EventParticipated/4.1-Event1/2-tu-vi-dai-viet.jpg)

#### Part 5 – The Hidden Iceberg of a Project: DevOps Before Disaster

Tran Minh Quan discussed the risks that lie "beneath the surface" of a software project when DevOps is not invested in properly from the start — emphasizing that most serious real-world project incidents stem from neglecting CI/CD pipelines, monitoring, and operational automation.

#### Part 6 – The Iceberg of Procrastination

Khac Uy Pham (3rd-year student, Vietnamese-German University) presented on *"When 'Laziness' is just the tip of fear,"* arguing that procrastination isn't simply laziness but often the visible tip of a deeper fear of failure or of not being good enough, and offered tips for recognizing and overcoming procrastination in study and project work.

![The Iceberg of Procrastination](/images/4-EventParticipated/4.1-Event1/3-iceberg-procrastination.jpg)

### Key Takeaways

- Learned about an engaging, visual way to learn AWS (AWS Cloud Quest) that can be used alongside the Bootcamp curriculum for extra practice.
- Understood that a personal project/startup (such as "Tử vi Đại Việt") considering a move to the cloud needs to carefully evaluate operating costs before migrating, rather than making the decision based on gut feeling.
- Gained a clearer sense of how important DevOps is from the very start of a project, to avoid accumulating "technical debt" that only gets addressed after an incident occurs.
- Learned a psychological perspective on confidence and procrastination — two factors that directly affect a student's study and work performance.
- Felt the community spirit and "learn while sharing" culture of the First Cloud AI Journey program.

### Applying to Work

- Trying out AWS Cloud Quest as a supplementary review channel alongside the Bootcamp modules.
- Applying the cost-evaluation-before-migration mindset (learned from the "Tử vi Đại Việt" case study) to analyzing costs for the CloudBrief final project.
- Bringing DevOps/CI-CD into consideration earlier in the design and deployment of the CloudBrief project, rather than focusing only on features.
- Applying the tips on "hacking" confidence and overcoming procrastination to preparing presentations and meeting team project deadlines.

### Event Experience

Attending the **First Cloud AI Journey – Workshop & Community Day** was a very engaging experience because the content was so varied — not just AWS technical topics, but also soft skills (confidence, overcoming procrastination) and real startup stories from fellow trainees. Hearing Nghia Tran share directly about his "Tử vi Đại Việt" product and his very real concerns when weighing a move to AWS helped me better understand how to apply what I've learned to a concrete business problem. The Hackathon session also motivated me to be more willing to take on challenging activities during my internship.
